# Instructor companion · Lab 15

## Teaching sequence

Before running, draw Maimonides' rule, predicted class size against enrolment, with its drops at 41, 81 and 121, and ask which variation in class size it supplies. Write the structural equation and the four moment conditions, and have students say which variables instrument themselves. Derive $(Z^\top X)^{-1}Z^\top y$, then the projection form, and show that $P_ZX$ leaves the intercept and both controls unchanged.

After the run, read the first stage before the 2SLS coefficients, confirm that the projection formula reproduces the native estimate, and spend time on the residual that enters $\hat\sigma^2$. Then compare classical and school-clustered standard errors (0.055 against 0.076): the instrument varies only between schools. Finish with the published comparison (−0.275 in Table IV against the rebuilt −0.277) and the 2019 follow-up that finds no effect in later data. Close on what the data cannot show: exclusion, and its dependence on the enrolment control (−0.158 without it).

## Worked exercise answers

**1.** The reduced-form `func1` coefficient is −0.15002 and the first-stage `func1` coefficient is 0.54154; their ratio, −0.27702, equals the 2SLS slope (indirect least squares). With more excluded instruments than endogenous regressors there would be one ratio per instrument, generally different, and 2SLS would combine them; the single ratio is the IV estimator only in the exactly identified case.

**2.** The hand-run second stage gives the same slope, −0.27702, but a standard error of 0.05362 against the classical 2SLS 0.05512. Both use the same matrix $(\hat X^\top\hat X)^{-1}$; they differ only in $\hat\sigma$. The structural residuals give $\hat\sigma=6.2486$ (residual sum of squares 78,676.7 over 2,015 degrees of freedom), while the fitted-class-size residuals give 6.0787 (74,456.3 over 2,015), so the standard-error ratio is $6.0787/6.2486=0.973$. Here the naive standard error is slightly too small; in other data the error can go either way and be much larger.

**3.** The classical first-stage $t$ statistic for `func1` is 25.882, and $25.882^2=669.85$, the Cragg–Donald F in the classical 2SLS output. The clustered $t$ is 14.761, and $14.761^2=217.88$, the Kleibergen–Paap F in the clustered output. With one excluded instrument each F is the square of the corresponding $t$. The numbers show that the rule strongly predicts class size given enrolment and disadvantage; they say nothing about whether the rule is correlated with the structural error.

**4.** If $y_i=\beta_0+\beta_dd_i+\beta_ee_i+\beta_xx_i+\gamma f_i+u_i$, the reduced-form coefficient on `func1` becomes $\beta_x\pi+\gamma$, where $\pi$ is the first-stage coefficient, so the IV slope converges to $\beta_x+\gamma/\pi$. With $\pi=0.5415$, $\gamma=0.05$ shifts the IV slope by $0.05/0.5415=0.092$, a third of the estimate. A stronger first stage (larger $\pi$) shrinks the shift but never removes it.

**5.** One class has no math score: no pupil took the math test, so the do-file sets its average to missing, and `missing="raise"` refuses to fit until it is dropped. On the remaining 2,018 classes the class-size coefficient is −0.2311, with classical standard error 0.0743 and school-clustered standard error 0.0986; percent disadvantaged is −0.3496 and enrolment 0.0410. Angrist and Lavy's Table IV reports −0.230 (0.092), −0.350 and 0.041 for the same specification, so the rebuild agrees to within 0.001, and the published Moulton-corrected standard error again lies between the classical and clustered ones.

**6.** For example: "In 2,019 fifth-grade classes in Israeli Jewish public schools tested in 1991, 2SLS with Maimonides' predicted class size as the excluded instrument, controlling for percent disadvantaged and enrolment, gives a class-size slope of −0.277 reading points per pupil (standard error 0.076 clustered by school, 1,001 degrees of freedom). The first stage is strong (clustered F = 218). The causal reading rests on the untestable assumption that the rule affects reading scores only through class size given enrolment and disadvantage, and it applies to classes whose size the rule moved; later Israeli data show no such effect."

**Misinterpretations to discuss.** Exclusion is an assumption about the mechanism, not proved by first-stage strength. Exactly identified IV supplies no overidentification test. Classical standard errors treat classes in one school as independent. The estimate depends on the enrolment control and on whether schools respond to the rule, and a 1991 estimate is not a permanent property of class size.
