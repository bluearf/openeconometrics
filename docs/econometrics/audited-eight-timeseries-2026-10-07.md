## U-MIDAS: unrestricted lag coefficients on a release-bound sample

The public sequence is `oe.midas_align(...)`, `oe.umidas(data=aligned, y=..., x=[...], covariance=...)`, then `oe.umidas_predict(restored, data=evaluation, kind="mean")`. `oe.umidas` estimates each supplied high-frequency lag independently through native OLS. This is the unrestricted lag-polynomial formulation discussed by [Foroni, Marcellino and Schumacher, Bundesbank Discussion Paper 35/2011](https://www.bundesbank.de/en/publications/research/discussion-papers/u-midas-midas-regressions-with-unrestricted-lag-polynomials-703636). The separate existing `oe.midas` route estimates normalized exponential-Almon weights by nonlinear least squares; its parameter and prediction laws are described in [the earlier MIDAS contract](tsworkflows.md#release-aware-midas).

Alignment supplies explicit low-frequency target dates and forecast origins, high-frequency observation dates, an exact calendar offset, lag count and, when declared, publication dates. Every lag's observation and release must be no later than its forecast origin. Lag zero is the latest released observation on the declared grid. Missing high-frequency calendar positions raise or are explicitly excluded by the alignment policy; they are never compressed into a shorter lag sequence. Publication dates cannot precede observations or reverse their order. Different vintages require separate alignment. The complete bound alignment, its hash and per-row dates are retained and checked again for estimation and prediction.

| Contract | Accepted U-MIDAS behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Sample and design | 3–256 unique numeric lags, at most 64 controls, optional intercept; actual `ModelFrame` missing policy and original physical positions retained. Full rank and positive residual df are required. A control named `Intercept` cannot collide with the intercept term. |
| Covariance and inference | Full nonrobust/HC0/HC1/HC2/HC3 covariance, including cross terms; coefficient SE, two-sided p and confidence intervals use native Student-t with df `N−K`. No HAC, cluster or sampling-weight interpretation is implied. |
| Inferential interpretation | Classical t inference is exact under independent Gaussian homoskedastic errors with a fixed full-rank design. HC t results use the native finite-df convention; they do not claim an exact robust finite-sample reference law. Serial dependence and model-selection uncertainty require separate methods. |
| Prediction | Saved coefficient order and full covariance produce mean SE from `x V x'`, followed by the same t convention. New rows need their own matching, release-safe alignment on the fitted frequency. `kind="outcome"` additionally requires nonrobust covariance and a positive finite saved residual scale, and assumes independent Gaussian outcome noise. Robust outcome intervals are refused. |
| Persistence | Ordinary `ModelSpec`/`ResultBundle`, complete alignment, lag terms and coefficients, physical sample alignment positions, full covariance/inference and data provenance. Prediction uses the saved fit and does not estimate parameters again. |
| Computation | Resident CPU float64; at most 20,000 fitted or evaluation rows. Estimation admits `N K² + K³ ≤ 100,000,000`; named design/QR/covariance and alignment buffers are subject to the configured workspace budget. Dataset collection, other devices and arbitrary weighted regressions are outside this route. |

The new independent tests compute all five covariance variants from NumPy least squares, leverage and residual formulas, then compare complete coefficient inference and saved mean intervals against SciPy's t distribution. They test physical missing positions, rank and reserved-term errors; stale values, changed calendars, future releases and missing alignment are rejected. A disabled OLS fitting function verifies that saved prediction does not refit. These are development oracles; production estimation does not import NumPy/SciPy estimators.

Earlier [temporal-disaggregation acceptance](temporal-disaggregation.md) remains baseline support: four original additive/proportional Denton forms, first-difference Denton-Cholette and fixed-rho Chow-Lin/Fernandez/Litterman. Complete December-end Y→Q/Y→M/Q→M calendars and `sum`/`mean`/`first`/`last` restrictions are explicit. Denton adjustments have no sampling SE/df/p/CI. The GLS routes retain full coefficient and latent-series covariance and conditional Gaussian Student-t inference with low-frequency df `M−P`; rho is supplied rather than estimated. Their at-most-240-high-period resource and release domains are separate from U-MIDAS. A release-aware regression is not itself a ragged-vintage nowcasting system, and none of these routes claims the broader Cholette–Dagum family or fitted-rho uncertainty.

## ETS: transparent bounded selection with complete initial-state information

`oe.ets(...)` fits one declared additive Gaussian innovations model. `oe.auto_ets(data=..., y=..., models=[...], period=..., criterion="aicc", candidate_options={...})` exhaustively compares a declared subset of `ANN`, `AAN`, `AdN`, `ANA`, `AAA`, `AdA`. The innovations state-space basis and its likelihood/forecast interpretation follow [Hyndman, Koehler, Snyder and Grose (2002)](https://robjhyndman.com/papers/hksg.pdf). The authors' [ETS estimation and selection description](https://otexts.com/fpp3/ets-estimation.html) includes estimated initial states and the noise scale in the information-criterion parameter count.

Every candidate uses the same ordered retained response and original physical positions. The forward calendar contract requires constant positive numeric spacing or an inferable regular datetime frequency. Endpoint exclusions are explicit; interior period deletion and irregular calendars fail. Declared per-model `fixed` smoothing parameters and known `initial` states are retained. Estimated seasonal initial states have the sum-to-zero identifying constraint. Coupled smoothing bounds, admissibility, parameter identification and observed-information geometry apply to each fitted model.

Write `K` for every free smoothing parameter, independent estimated initial-state parameter and sigma, and `N` for the common response periods. The Gaussian likelihood retains its constants:

```text
AIC  = −2 log L + 2 K
BIC  = −2 log L + K log N
AICc = AIC + 2 K (K+1) / (N−K−1)
```

AICc cannot be used when `N≤K+1`. Actual successful-candidate K is the complete reported coefficient count; fixed smoothing parameters and known initial states are not counted as estimated. Estimated initial-state/sigma covariance includes all off-diagonal entries and is transformed to physical reporting units. Coefficient SE, z statistics, p and confidence intervals use asymptotic normal likelihood inference, rather than residual-df Student-t calibration. Selection and post-selection coefficient inference condition on the selected model.

Each candidate record preserves its options, status, fitted spec, likelihood and all applicable IC, N/K, ordered complete coefficients, full covariance and convergence diagnostics. Failures retain typed errors and their settings. A sample mismatch aborts comparison; a completely failed search raises `no_valid_model` with its complete `candidates` history. An exact criterion tie selects the first caller-declared candidate. The returned object is an ordinary ETS `ResultBundle` with its selected `ModelSpec` and complete `extra.auto_selection` history, rather than a different forecasting estimator.

`oe.forecast(restored, steps)` propagates future innovations under the saved selected ETS model. The normal pointwise intervals include future innovation uncertainty conditional on fitted parameters and the fitted terminal state. They exclude estimated smoothing/initial-state/sigma uncertainty and model-selection uncertainty. Initial states are part of fitted-parameter covariance, but that covariance is not injected into conditional forecast intervals. This distinction matters even when the coefficient covariance and future innovation calculations are correct; no unconditional coverage across selected models is asserted.

Search accepts 1–6 distinct candidates, seasonal period 2–48 and at most 20,000 response periods. `max_candidates`, `max_work`, per-fit bounds and named retained-response/candidate-history workspace plans are checked. Search `work_units` sum conservative candidate `N × state_width × K_upper_bound²` information geometries. These units exclude optimizer iteration operations, which are bounded separately by `max_iterations`. They are not elapsed-time estimates or an exact total-operation count. Multiplicative components, weighted fits, streaming Dataset and non-CPU devices are unsupported here.

The independent acceptance fixture fixes smoothing coefficients in all six models and computes the scalar innovations recurrence, least-squares initial-state optimum, Gaussian sigma ML, full initial-state/sigma information and each IC separately. It also checks failure retention, typed resource rejection, full JSON restoration and origin-local repeated searches. The retained broader ETS tests cover free smoothing parameters, transformation covariance and future innovation variances; the fixed-smoothing oracle does not replace that evidence.

## Training origins, selection and release isolation

`oe.rolling(spec, data=..., window=..., step=..., forecast_steps=..., evaluate=True)` and `oe.recursive(spec, data=..., minimum=...)` record half-open training windows, origin positions, actual physical input/sample positions, fitted coefficients and full covariance/inference, metrics, specs and failures. Future observed outcomes enter scoring only after fitting and forecasting. Future regressors are supplied separately by origin. Reverse modes are retrospective and refuse prospective forecast/scoring claims.

For MIDAS/U-MIDAS, each window slices and rebinds the dated alignment to its own rows. Window provenance records the original positions and parent data hash separately from the training sample hash. Changing data outside a training window can therefore change the parent provenance while leaving that window's bound sample and fitted numerical result unchanged.

For automatic ETS, use `rolling(..., selection={"models": ..., "criterion": ..., "candidate_options": ...})`. This reruns every declared candidate inside each training origin, retaining the complete per-origin search. Refitting a previously selected ETS `ModelSpec` alone only refits that selected model. The new tests perturb later response values and later released high-frequency inputs and compare the earlier origin's complete coefficients, covariance, inference, metrics and selection history. No future-data invariance is inferred merely from matching forecast means.

## Canonical proper-prior state space with masks and schedules

The current public routes are `oe.sspace(...)`, `oe.sspace_filter(...)`, `oe.sspace_smooth(result)`, `oe.sspace_autocov(result)`, `oe.sspace_disturbances(result)` and `oe.forecast(restored, steps, future=...)`. The canonical independent-shock equations are:

```text
y[t]   = Z[t] a[t] + d[t] + e[t],   Cov(e[t]) = H[t]
a[t+1] = T[t] a[t] + c[t] + u[t],   Cov(u[t]) = Q[t]
```

`a0,P0` describe the proper prior before the first measurement. The full multivariate measurement is the named `y` plus `responses`. H is positive definite; Q and P0 may be positive semidefinite. Known initialization permits the accepted nonexplosive transition domain. Stationary initialization solves the exact mean/Lyapunov equations with a strictly stable, time-invariant T/Q/c and no state exogenous path; time-invariant free parameters can still be fitted. Observation schedules are permitted under that initialization. No large-variance approximation is labelled exact diffuse inference.

With `missing="mask"`, partial measurements use the observed principal subvector and its full H block. An entirely missing date contributes zero to likelihood and still applies its outgoing transition. All dates remain in calendar order. Dates and known exogenous paths must themselves be complete. Missing observation/innovation cells persist as JSON null; masks, per-period counts and observed-cell totals persist separately. N for AIC/BIC is the retained period count, including entirely missing dates, and the observed measurement count is also reported. This is an explicit IC convention rather than a claim that cell and period penalties coincide.

`system["schedules"]` supplies exact T/Z/Q/H/c/d values per original physical row. Sorting by time reorders these arrays identically. T[t], c[t], Q[t] propagate after measurement t, including the final outgoing transition into the saved forecast origin. No calendar hole is inferred or compressed. Known state/measurement exogenous mappings have saved column order and fixed coefficient matrices. A free parameter cannot occupy an explicitly scheduled matrix. Supported static free cells use identity, exp-positive or tanh-unit transforms; symmetric covariance aliases and duplicate declarations are checked.

Filtering uses the exact observed-cell Gaussian prediction-error likelihood and Joseph covariance updates. ML uses analytic Torch score/Hessian and the full inverse observed information transformed to physical parameter units, including cross terms. Free inferential parameters require identification, convergence and positive information. Coefficient SE/z/p/CI use asymptotic normal inference; known fixed systems have no invented coefficient tests or residual-df law. Independent process and measurement shocks are the model assumption; a nonzero posterior disturbance cross covariance is a conditional result, not an estimated contemporaneous structural correlation.

The full fixed-interval RTS covariance conditions on all observed measurements with fixed system parameters. Its timing and gain agree with the RTS recursion on [Särkkä's 2014 author tutorial, slide 78 (PDF page index 77)](https://www.eurasip.org/Seminars/Tutorials/EUSIPCO2014%20Tutorial%20Bayesian%20Filtering%20and%20Smoothing.pdf). Current PSD-compatible solves check numerical rank and nullspace compatibility using a scale-dependent threshold; they add no ridge, PSD projection or eigenvalue clipping. This includes accepted deterministic proper-state geometry, rather than an obsolete strict-PD-only smoother restriction.

| Saved helper | Complete conditional output |
| --- | --- |
| `sspace_smooth` | All state means and full within-period smoothed covariance, with saved periods and masks |
| `sspace_autocov` | N−1 full blocks `Cov(a[t+1], a[t] | all observed Y)`; next/current orientation is explicit |
| `sspace_disturbances` | Process/measurement means, full individual covariance, cross blocks and joint covariance. Correlated missing measurement noise is conditioned on the observed noise subvector. The unobserved last outgoing process shock retains zero mean and Q[last]. |
| `forecast` | Full per-horizon state/measurement means and covariance; first-response normal pointwise interval. Every fitted scheduled key and known exogenous column needs a complete exact future path. The actual last retained calendar period and physical row identify the origin. |

Forecasting starts from the saved next state after the last fitted outgoing transition. The first future measurement uses its supplied Z/H/d before the next future outgoing T/Q/c. Conditional state and future shock uncertainty are included; parameter uncertainty and simultaneous cross-horizon bands are not claimed. The [official state-space representation](https://www.statsmodels.org/stable/statespace.html) and [Helske's KFAS author paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1612.01907) provide equation/missingness references. Production calculation uses the native engine, and source oracles directly condition dense joint Gaussian distributions rather than delegating estimation to those packages.

The three smoothing/disturbance helpers accept a `ResultBundle`, its JSON object or JSON string. Complete model equations/templates, parameter covariance, observations, masks, periods, sample positions, filtered/next moments and provenance are saved. Integrity/geometry checks apply before replay, with a digest over the complete finite canonical JSON bundle excluding only `extra.state_sha256`. That digest detects accidental state changes; it is not authentication. Helpers replay filtering/smoothing calculations from saved parameter values without optimizing a new model. Restored forecasts similarly use the saved model and explicit future paths.

Resource admission is resident CPU float64 with at most 20,000 periods, 16 states, 8 measurements and 20 free parameters, plus bounded known exogenous widths. Matrix/path/Lyapunov/derivative/smoothing/forecast geometries have explicit 50-million work gates and named workspace plans. Forecast horizons are 1–10,000 subject to those geometry limits. Dataset collection, weights, CUDA/MPS, singular H, exact diffuse, nonlinear systems, automatic DFM/DSGE construction and parameter-uncertain forecasts remain outside this core.

The current independent small-system fixture builds the complete joint Gaussian distribution, masks its observed cells, and compares exact likelihood, full conditional state covariance and next/current lag blocks for static and scheduled systems. Saved-state replay is exercised with the fitting function disabled, and changed observations fail the integrity check. Retained canonical tests additionally cover all six schedules, known exogenous paths, outgoing forecast timing, posterior disturbance cross blocks, singular compatible state geometry, physical transformed-information covariance and rejected domains. Direct analytical agreement is distinct from licensed Stata/EViews execution.

## Runnable complete-state artifacts and evidence boundaries

[The runnable example](../examples/audited_eight_timeseries.py) exposes `run(destination)` and a direct script entry. It writes exact JSON bytes for four fitted `ResultBundle`s before postestimation, validates their complete readback and hashes the actual saved files. Ten complete output states and their LaTeX exports retain U-MIDAS prediction/refits, automatic ETS forecasting/reselection, canonical smoothing/lag/disturbance/forecast moments, ML coefficients and a baseline Denton benchmark. The canonical bundle digest is independently recomputed from the saved complete file. The example's synthetic seeded inputs demonstrate calculation and persistence within these domains, rather than vendor or population accuracy.
