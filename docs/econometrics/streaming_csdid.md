# Full-source replay CSDID

`csdid` accepts a replayable balanced-panel `Dataset` without collecting the observation matrix or the unit-by-ATT influence matrix in RAM. It implements the existing OpenEconometrics model's outcome-regression (`reg`), normalized inverse-probability (`ipw`) and doubly robust (`dr`) group-time effects. Controls can be never treated or not yet treated; the pre-treatment base can vary or be universal. Covariates use their base-period values and globally declared treatment coding. Without covariates, all three methods reduce to differences in mean changes.

The estimands and aggregations follow [Callaway and Sant'Anna (2021)](https://psantanna.com/files/Callaway_SantAnna_2020.pdf). The comparison and base-period convention is documented by the authors' [att_gt reference](https://bcallaway11.github.io/did/reference/att_gt.html). This implementation reports analytic unit-clustered influence covariance and pointwise normal intervals. It does not implement the authors' default multiplier bootstrap, uniform bands, repeated cross-sections or user weights. Causal interpretation still requires the stated parallel-trends, overlap and treatment-timing assumptions; the numerical adapter does not test those assumptions by completing a fit.

Exact unit/period keys and encoded response/covariate records live in owned SQLite storage. Each ATT(g,t) nuisance fit and stacked analytic Jacobian scans all participating paired unit records. Native float64 Torch TSQR solves the outcome regression; native Newton and a globally checked separation certificate fit the propensity model. Unit influence contributions, including fitted nuisance uncertainty, are stored on disk. A bounded unit-block TSQR of the ATT and cohort-share influences gives their joint covariance. Simple, dynamic, cohort and calendar aggregations include uncertainty in the estimated cohort shares; nuisance parameters and cohort shares are not held fixed when computing standard errors.

Whole units already treated in the initial period are excluded, with an explicit note and effective physical-position hash. Later-treated units can act as never-treated controls. Every retained unit must have one observation at every observed period. First-treatment time must be constant within units, including missing never-treated status. Integer period identity is retained exactly in signed int64, including identities beyond float64's integer range; floating period inputs must be exact integers within their representable domain. Datetime time is ranked in sorted order, with the existing warning that gaps cannot be detected. Missing policies, category declarations and the final complete-source fingerprint are checked independently of stored paired records.

The workspace plan bounds decoded records, local nuisance derivatives, stored comparison metadata, row score blocks and joint factors. Joint ATT plus cohort-share output is limited to 384 columns; outcome/propensity estimating equations have the same bounded parameter budget. These are model-width and workspace limits, not total-row ceilings. Panel storage and sparse unit influences use disk proportional to panel rows and participating unit-comparison pairs. The free-disk check uses a conservative estimate; enough disk, repeated-scan time and suitable hardware remain necessary. Temporary storage is cleaned on success and failure. At most 400 rows enter reporting metadata; they do not replace any fitted unit.

Verification includes all reg/IPW/DR × never/notyet × varying/universal combinations with and without covariates; categorical coding, missing complete-unit deletion, early/late treatment, within-comparison collinearity, exact integer/date periods, reader partition and unit-rescaling invariance, CPU context restoration, source mutation and resource failures. Independent NumPy estimating-equation finite differences verify nuisance uncertainty; independent unit influence and cohort-share derivatives verify joint and aggregate covariance. NumPy is a development-only oracle. Results preserve JSON and publication-table LaTeX contracts.
