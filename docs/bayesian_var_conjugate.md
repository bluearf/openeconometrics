# Proper conjugate Bayesian VAR

This implementation supplies a bounded conditional VAR with one proper matrix-normal/inverse-Wishart (MNIW) prior. The method's child is MARKET-734; its broader Bayesian parent remains open. Shared public integration and targeted tests are implemented in the current integration branch. The bounded original-author deterministic posterior comparison under GNU Octave has passed; installed BVAR application evidence, final current-head/base full hosted gates and merge remain separate pending delivery gates. No completed Stata parity claim is made.

For ordered series `y1,...,ym`, the model conditions on the first `p` observations and uses rows of

`x_t = [1, y_(t-1)', ..., y_(t-p)']` (the declared intercept may be omitted),

`Y = X B + E`, `E_t | Sigma ~ N_m(0,Sigma)` independently across time. The full proper prior is explicitly supplied:

`Sigma ~ IW_m(S0,nu0)`, `B | Sigma ~ MN_(k,m)(M0,V0,Sigma)`.

`V0` and `S0` must be finite, exactly symmetric positive definite matrices and `nu0 > m-1`. No prior scale or shrinkage default is inferred from the data. A Minnesota prior that is explicitly represented in this full conjugate MNIW form is a possible caller specification. Fixed-Sigma Minnesota, independent-normal/IW, equationwise normal/inverse-gamma, Jeffreys/improper priors and stochastic volatility are different models and are unsupported here. Collinear lag designs remain in full: a proper prior gives a nonsingular posterior without dropping terms.

The six numerical APIs are `bayes_var`, `bayes_var_predict`, `bayes_var_contrast`, `bayes_var_draws`, `bayes_var_forecast`, and `bayes_var_irf`. They are exposed lazily from `openecon`, together with their fourteen typed declarations, seven explicit restore routes, `bayes_var_tables` and `bayes_var_state_json`: 29 public exports in the dedicated `bayesian_var_public` family. That family has no generic ModelSpec estimator, ResultBundle or common predict/forecast registration. `bayes_var` requires a resident DataFrame, 2–4 complete real numeric series, 1–4 lags, an explicit separate integral `time` column, and 1–10,000 original rows with more than `p` rows. No exogenous variables, weights, missing-data imputation, Dataset collection, GPU fitting, stationary truncation, stability resampling, infinite-horizon moments or FEVD are implemented.

Time is declared as consecutive signed 64-bit integer periods and one of `unit`, `daily`, `monthly`, `quarterly` or `annual`. Labels do not infer a frequency. Sorting uses the complete original integer periods; the original ordering, every modeled source cell, declared dtype, exact row index and complete time permutation are saved. The initially accepted index codec is RangeIndex, plain NumPy integral Index, plain object Index with bounded string/integer labels, and complete timezone-naive DatetimeIndex with exact unit/frequency/name. MultiIndex, categorical/nullable/Arrow/string-extension indices, timezone-aware dates, PeriodIndex, floating indices and arbitrary objects are refused. Source and index constructor equality is validated after budget admission and before any tensor, numerical factor or RNG side effect. Original model columns support explicit NumPy and pandas nullable real numeric dtypes with no missing cells; integral series cells must be exactly representable in float64.

The guards apply to the resident original source, query matrices, primitive arrays, joint covariance, saved metadata, serialization copies and planned work. Defaults are 128 MiB workspace, 100 million conservative work units, 2 million stored numeric values, at most 10,000 retained draws and horizon 24. Full fixed-query covariance makes the admitted query row count smaller than the resident source cap. These are implementation/resource limits; they are not statistical sample-size requirements. The configured workspace budget can lower the accepted size. It bounds planned buffers and serialization copies, not total process RSS.

## Exact posterior and queries

Let `K_N=V0^-1+X'X`, `V_N=K_N^-1`, and

`M_N=V_N(V0^-1 M0+X'Y)`.

The implementation computes

`S_N=S0+(Y-X M_N)'(Y-X M_N)+(M_N-M0)'V0^-1(M_N-M0)`

and `nu_N=nu0+n` using native CPU float64 factors, with no jitter or covariance repair. The posterior is the full joint MNIW law. The marginal coefficient distribution is **matrix-t**; it is not in general a generic elliptical multivariate t on `vec(B)`.

Every rank-one contrast `l' B r` has scalar Student t degrees of freedom `d=nu_N-m+1`, location `l'M_N r`, and squared scale `(l'V_N l)(r'S_N r)/d`. Individual coefficient intervals are special cases. Arbitrary multi-coefficient contrasts need joint draws or the matrix-t law rather than a substituted generic multivariate t. Zero rank-one contrasts are exact point masses.

When moments exist, coefficient ordering is column-major `vec(B)` and innovation ordering is lower-column-major `vech(Sigma)`. `E(Sigma)=S_N/(nu_N-m-1)` requires `nu_N>m+1`; `Cov(vec(B))=E(Sigma) ⊗ V_N` includes every cross-equation block. `Cov(vech(Sigma))` requires `nu_N>m+3`. The complete joint covariance is supplied only when all its moments exist. The zero coefficient/innovation cross-covariance block does not mean their posterior laws are independent. Location fields remain separate from moment fields. Undefined moments are explicit `None`, never estimated from a finite set of draws.

`bayes_var_predict(result, design, terms=result.terms)` requires complete declared fixed design rows in exactly the saved term order. Each row's full multivariate predictive law is t with `d`, location `x'M_N`, and scale `(1+x'V_N x)S_N/d`. Across fixed rows, the joint law is matrix-t with row scale `I+X_q V_N X_q'`; the saved full cross-row/cross-equation covariance, when available, uses that full matrix. These fixed rows are not a recursive multi-step forecast. `bayes_var_contrast` saves its original left/right vectors, threshold, alpha and exact t result.

All posterior and derived states are frozen typed objects with complete nested sources/parents, hashes, full arrays, primitive inputs and explicit declarations. JSON reload, validated copies, summary and public queries recheck the canonical source and cached scientific algebra. Hashes detect accidental corruption; they are not authentication. A refreshed hash cannot rescue changed cached moments. Primitive replay is portable algebra; saved RNG version/algorithm/seed describe the generating runtime and do not assert cross-version seed reproducibility.

## Joint draws and finite-horizon uncertainty

Native Bartlett factors generate inverse-Wishart innovations; standard matrix-normal primitives then generate coefficients conditional on that same innovation covariance. A local seeded CPU generator leaves global RNG state untouched. Every generated draw, its primitives and companion spectral radius/stability classification are saved. Unstable draws are retained, including when reporting finite-horizon forecasts and IRFs. Numerical overflow is an explicit operation failure; no draw is clipped, omitted or replaced.

A recursive forecast uses one common `B,Sigma` draw across the whole path. It saves both the parameter-only conditional-mean path and the outcome path with future correlated Gaussian innovations, along with the complete standard innovation primitives. Simple IRFs use lag-polynomial recursion; orthogonalized IRFs multiply by the ordered lower Cholesky innovation factor, so their interpretation depends on the saved series order. Empirical intervals are pointwise equal-tail quantiles, with no joint-band coverage claim.

### Why the moment guards differ

Write `d=nu_N-m+1`. Under `IW_m(S_N,nu_N)`, each diagonal entry has inverse-gamma shape `d/2`, so `E((tr Sigma)^r)` is finite for `r<d/2`. This follows from the diagonal moments and a finite-sum power bound. Since Sigma is positive semidefinite, its operator norm is bounded by its trace. Conditional coefficients have a representation `B=M_N+L_V Z L_Sigma'`, with Gaussian `Z`, so their degree-`s` norm moments are bounded by a constant times `1+||Sigma||^(s/2)`.

At finite forecast horizon `H`, the path is a polynomial of degree at most `H` in B. Each future-noise term has at most `H-1` coefficient factors and one `sqrt(Sigma)` factor. A sufficient bound for its second moment is therefore a constant times `1+||Sigma||^H`, giving the strict guard **`nu_N > m+2H-1`**. A sufficient mean guard is `nu_N>m+H-1`; a sufficient fourth-moment guard is `nu_N>m+4H-1`.

A simple IRF at lag `h` has at most `h` coefficient factors, giving the sufficient second-moment guard `nu_N>m+2h-1`. Its lag-zero identity is constant and has every moment regardless of this conservative bound. An orthogonalized IRF `Phi_h chol(Sigma)` has an **additional square-root Sigma factor**. Its second moment is bounded by `1+||Sigma||^(h+1)`, giving **`nu_N>m+2h+1`**. Its sufficient mean guard is `nu_N>m+h`, and its fourth-moment guard is `nu_N>m+4h+3`. At lag zero, its second moment already needs `E(Sigma)`, as expected. These are sufficient uniform bounds, not claims that every particular contrast fails outside them.

Multi-step and IRF covariance fields are named `covariance_mc_estimate` and explicitly labeled IID Monte Carlo estimates. They are exposed only when the appropriate sufficient population second-moment guard passes and at least two draws exist. Mean MCSE uses that second-moment guard. Covariance MCSE is an asymptotic centered-product influence-function plug-in and additionally requires the fourth-moment guard; it is not a certified error bound. When a guard fails, moments/MC errors remain `None`, even though a finite array has finite empirical variance. Quantiles and every retained draw remain available. No estimate is presented as an exact analytic population covariance.
